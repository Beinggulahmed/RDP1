## 4:18 — Ending: what the film reveals (n05)

*Picture: carnival; Woody walking home whistling; the Coronet "The End" card (film 10:04–10:22, 12:02–12:25, muted). Lower-thirds: "Median age at first marriage, 2024: women 28.6 · men 30.5"; "Copyright registered 1949 (MP5069) · No renewal found in the 1976–1977 Catalog of Copyright Entries".*

**Our narrator:**
> So what does this film reveal about America in 1949? A country just out of a war wanted order, and it wanted it early: marriage in your early twenties, after a courtship run by rules everyone could recite. [S13, S16] The year before, Alfred Kinsey's report on male sexual behavior had become a best seller by documenting how far real behavior strayed from the script. [S18] Today the median American first marries at around thirty for men and twenty-eight for women. [S17] The film itself survives in the public domain, because no renewal of its 1949 copyright has ever been found. [S24, COPYRIGHT_REPORT.md] A reminder that the good night at the door was never only about manners. It was about who got to decide.

## Fact-check notes (claims deliberately left out)

- Director: Wikipedia credits Gilbert Altschul, TCM credits Ted Peshak → not stated.
- Cast names (Jackie Gleason / John Lindsay listings are contradictory) → not stated.
- Coronet founding year (1934 vs 1941 vs 1946 in different sources) → not stated; "of Glenview, Illinois" is stated instead.
- The "70 % more films than the other three producers" figure is a Coronet advertisement → not used in narration.
- Kinsey sales figures vary (200,000–500,000) → narration says only "best seller".
- "Youngest ever recorded": the 1950 figures (20.3/22.8) are within 0.2 years of the series minimum (1956: 20.1/22.5) and lower than every year before 1940 in the Census series from 1890 → phrased as "among the youngest the Census Bureau has ever recorded".
