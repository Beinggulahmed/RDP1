# COPYRIGHT_REPORT.md — rights verification for *Dating: Do's and Don'ts* (1949)

**Conclusion (summary):** The 1949 Coronet film *Dating: Do's and Don'ts* is, on the
evidence below, a United States work whose copyright was registered in 1949 and
**not renewed** in its 28th year (1976–1977). Under U.S. law a work published
1923–1963 whose copyright was not renewed is in the public domain. The Internet
Archive (Prelinger Archives) and Wikimedia Commons both label the film public
domain. Commercial use, editing, remastering and redistribution of the footage
and soundtrack are therefore permitted, with the residual uncertainties noted in
section 6. The rights verification was judged sufficient to proceed with
production.

Verification date: 2026-10-13. Evidence files are under `source/evidence/`
(fetched automatically by `.github/workflows/fetch-archival-source.yml`).

## 2. Internet Archive / Prelinger statements

- The item page shows **Usage: Public Domain** and the Prelinger collection
  classification. The Prelinger Archives state that reuse of their films on the
  Internet Archive is allowed according to the licence shown on each film's page,
  and that material in the public domain may be reused without charge or
  permission (Prelinger Archives site; Rick Prelinger declaration in
  *MGM v. Grokster*, 2002: about 60 % of the collection is public domain). Wikipedia
  ("Prelinger Archives") summarises: over 8,500 public-domain films are available
  for download and unrestricted reuse.
- Internet Archive hosting is **not** itself evidence of PD status; the Archive's
  terms (`archive_org_terms.html`) place the responsibility on the user. The
  Prelinger label is however an informed curatorial statement by the collection
  that owns the physical print, not a random upload.

## 3. U.S. Copyright Office records — Catalog of Copyright Entries (CCE)

The fetch workflow downloaded the OCR text of every CCE *Motion Pictures*
volume on the Internet Archive and searched it for the title (results in
`source/evidence/rights/cce_grep_results.txt`).

**Original registration found — CCE Third Series, Vol. 4, Parts 12–13, Motion
Pictures and Filmstrips, Jan–Dec 1950** (`catalogofcopyri341213libr`):

> DATING: DO'S AND DON'TS. Coronet, 1949. 13 min., sd., b&w, 16mm.
> Summary: Presents teen-agers' dating problems and suggests how they should be [solved …]

with the registration number **MP5069** in the volume's index. (The registration
describes the b&w release print; Coronet sold both colour and b&w prints of its
titles. The Kodachrome print used here is the same work.)

**Renewal search — no renewal found.** A 1949 registration had to be renewed in
its 28th year, i.e. in 1976 or 1977 (renewal could also be filed up to the end of
1977 for a work registered late in 1949). The renewal sections of the following
volumes were searched and contain **no entry** for the title:

**Related record (not a renewal):** CCE 1973 (`catalogofc19733271213libr`) lists

film) with its own copyright; it does not extend protection to the 1949 original,
whose footage is what is used here. **The 1972 edition must not be used.**

## 5. Third-party material inside the film

- **Music:** the original soundtrack contains an orchestral score typical of
  Coronet productions (library/commissioned music). No separate music credit is
  visible on this print (titles missing). Such embedded music is part of the
  registered motion-picture work; with the film's copyright unrenewed, the U.S.
  copyright in the film as published has expired. Separately-registered
  underlying musical compositions could in theory have their own term; no such
  registration was identified, and the score is used only as it occurs in the
  excerpts (≈ 2.5 minutes total of original audio).
- **Performers:** the actors are uncredited teenagers from 1949. No rights of
  publicity issue arises from documentary use of a published film.
- **Trademarks:** the Coronet crown logo appears on the "The End" card; it is
  shown only as part of the archival footage (nominative/documentary use).

## 6. Residual uncertainty and how it was handled

1. **OCR coverage of renewal volumes.** The CCE search relies on OCR text; a badly
   scanned line could hide an entry. Mitigation: all three candidate years
   (1976, 1977, 1978) and adjacent years were searched, with a fuzzy pattern
   (`dating[^a-z]{0,6}do`). The Copyright Office's online catalogue only covers
   1978 onward and a 1978 search of the printed catalogue found nothing. A paid
   Copyright Office search report would make this conclusive; it was not
   purchased (per project rules).
2. **Country of origin.** The work is a U.S. work; non-U.S. use is governed by
   each country's term (e.g. in life + 70 countries the term may still run,
   because the directors died later than 1956). Commercial distribution outside
   the United States should be assessed per territory.
3. **Incomplete print.** The missing head titles mean the print cannot show a
   © notice; this does not affect the renewal analysis.
4. **The 1972 remake** (*The Dating Scene*) is separately copyrighted and is not
   used.
