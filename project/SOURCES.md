## A. The film itself

- **S1.** Internet Archive item *Dating: Do's and Don'ts* (Coronet Instructional Films, 1949), identifier `DatingDo1949`, Prelinger Archives collection. https://archive.org/details/DatingDo1949 — metadata JSON saved at `source/ia_metadata.json` (fetched by `.github/workflows/fetch-archival-source.yml`). ## B. Coronet Instructional Films

- **S6.** Wikipedia, "Coronet Films". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronet_Films — founded by David A. Smart (publisher of *Esquire* and *Coronet* magazines) with his brothers; first copyrighted titles 1941; studio in Glenview, Illinois; best known for post-war social-guidance films on dating, family life, courtesy and citizenship; many titles shot in colour Kodachrome; company closed 1997, library now owned by Phoenix Learning Group.
- **S7.** WTTW Chicago Stories, "Those Films You Saw in School". https://www.wttw.com/a/main.taf-p=1,7,1,1,15.html — Coronet originated the social-guidance genre; competitors such as Encyclopaedia Britannica Films followed.
- **S8.** JSTOR Daily, "Are You Popular?" https://daily.jstor.org/are-you-popular — Coronet began with military training films, then pivoted to general educational films after World War II.
- **S9.** *See and Hear: the journal on audio-visual learning* (1951–53), Coronet advertisement claiming it had produced "70 percent more 16mm instructional films since World War II than the other three leading producers combined" (promotional claim, cited as such). https://lantern.mediahist.org/catalog/seehearjournal195153oneaucrich_0184
- **S10.** New York Sun obituary, "Ted Peshak, 87, Directed 'Mental Hygiene' Films of 1950s". https://www.nysun.com/article/obituaries-ted-peshak-87-directed-mental-hygiene-films

## C. The genre and its critics

- **S11.** Ken Smith, *Mental Hygiene: Classroom Films 1945–1970* (Blast Books, 1999). Smith calls the films a "postwar morality offensive by educators, psychologists and the clergy" (as quoted by SourceWatch, https://www.sourcewatch.org/index.php/Coronet_Instructional_Films) and lists *Dating Do's and Don'ts* among the best of the dating films. Reviews: Education Week, "Morality Play" (2000) https://www.edweek.org/education/morality-play/2000/03 ; Al Filreis, "'Mental Hygiene': The Dos and Don'ts of the Doo-Wop Age" https://writing.upenn.edu/~afilreis/50s/hygiene-films.html
- **S12.** Ken Smith in the *New York Times* (2000), paraphrased in reviews: there would have been no need for mental-hygiene films if the young really had behaved so pleasantly.

## D. Dating history and demography

- **S13.** Beth L. Bailey, *From Front Porch to Back Seat: Courtship in Twentieth-Century America* (Johns Hopkins University Press, 1988). Pre-war "rating and dating" valued a "promiscuous popularity" measured by the number and variety of dates; after the war the ideal shifted to going steady, which by the early 1950s "most fifteen-year-olds did do" — a kind of play-marriage. Summaries: https://press.jhu.edu/books/title/1626/front-porch-back-seat ; American Heritage, "Front Porch to Back Seat" https://www.americanheritage.com/front-porch-back-seat
- **S14.** Willard Waller, "The Rating and Dating Complex", *American Sociological Review* 2 (1937): 727–734 — the campus "rating and dating" system.
- **S15.** Wikipedia, "Going steady". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Going_steady — the practice grew in prevalence in the United States after World War II; a 1951 study called it "a national problem"; *Life* magazine covered it with alarm in 1957.
- **S16.** U.S. Census Bureau, Table MS-2, "Estimated Median Age at First Marriage, by Sex: 1890 to Present" (CPS data from 1947). 1950: men 22.8, women 20.3; the all-time low was 1956 (22.5 / 20.1). https://www.census.gov/data/tables/time-series/demo/families/marital.html ; Minnesota Legislative Reference Library fact sheet reproducing the series https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2010/other/100512.pdf
- **S17.** Bowling Green State University, National Center for Family & Marriage Research, "Median Age at First Marriage, 2024" (ACS): women 28.6, men 30.5 (USAFacts, using CPS, gives 30.2 / 28.6). https://www.bgsu.edu/ncfmr/resources/data/family-profiles/fp-26-01.html ; https://usafacts.org/articles/state-relationships-marriages-and-living-alone-us/
- **S18.** Alfred C. Kinsey et al., *Sexual Behavior in the Human Male* (W. B. Saunders, January 1948) — an immediate best-seller (about 200,000 hardback copies within months), published the year before the film. https://www.biography.com/scientists/alfred-kinsey ; https://bigthink.com/the-past/kinsey-75-sexologist-scandalized-america/
- **S19.** Reuben Hill, *Families Under Stress: Adjustment to the Crises of War Separation and Reunion* (Harper, 1949); Hill was at the University of North Carolina 1949–1957 and later president of the International Sociological Association. https://www.isa-sociology.org/en/about-isa/history-of-isa/isa-past-presidents/list-of-presidents/reuben-hill
- **S20.** Digital Public Library of America exhibition, "Teenage Culture" (postwar America): the word "teenager" entered common use in the 1940s, promoted by advertisers; *Seventeen* magazine launched in 1944. https://dp.la/exhibitions/children-progressive-era/childhood-postwar-america/teenage-culture
